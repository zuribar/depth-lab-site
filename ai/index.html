More about this service

The real-time AI mirror: live AI video from a camera

A client asked for a real-time AI mirror: you stand in front of a camera and see yourself in another world, with everything running locally on one GPU. The deal did not close. The project had become interesting, so Zori kept working on it.

What the R&D clips show

In the R&D clips, a man stands in an ordinary living room, and in the AI image the floor around him has turned into sand and shallow surf. He crouches to put his hands in the water and kicks at the small waves, and the AI image moves with him. Before-and-after clips show the camera image next to the same moment after the AI.

From about 5 to about 10 FPS

The hard part is speed. Video AI models render clips in minutes, and a mirror needs several new frames every second. The first version ran at about 5 FPS. After optimizing the pipeline and training his own LoRA, Zori reached about 10 FPS on the same GPU. The work is still in progress.

AI installations for venues, malls and museums

Zori sees the mirror as an AI installation for entertainment venues, malls and museums. Visitors become part of the content: each person who steps in front of the camera sees a live image of themselves in another world.

AI in the content pipeline

Zori uses AI every day. The studio produces AI-generated content on local and cloud pipelines, and uses AI coding agents to automate parts of the pipeline, such as Unreal scripting, rendering and encoding.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a real-time AI mirror installation in Israel?

DepthLab, the Tel Aviv studio of Zori Baazov, is developing one. In the R&D version you stand in front of a camera and see yourself in another world, running locally on one GPU at about 10 FPS. The work is still in progress.

What is real-time AI video, and why is it hard?

Real-time AI video, also called live AI video, is video in which an AI model changes the live camera image as it happens, for example turning a living room floor into sand and sea around the person standing in it. The hard part is speed: video AI models usually render clips in minutes, and a live installation needs several new frames every second. The DepthLab mirror reaches about 10 FPS on one local GPU.

Can AI video run in real time on one local GPU?

Yes, at a limited frame rate. The first version of the DepthLab mirror ran at about 5 FPS. After optimizing the pipeline and training his own LoRA, Zori reached about 10 FPS on the same GPU.

What is the difference between an AI mirror and an AI photo booth?

An AI photo booth takes a picture and returns an edited image after a short wait. A real-time AI mirror shows a live, moving image, so you see yourself in another world while you move. That needs several new frames every second, which makes it much harder to build.

Why train a custom LoRA?

A LoRA is a small add-on trained on top of an AI model to teach it a specific look or behavior. In the mirror R&D, training his own LoRA was part of the work that took the speed from about 5 FPS to about 10 FPS on the same GPU.
