Real-Time AI Mirror

DepthLab, a Tel Aviv studio, is developing a real-time AI mirror for entertainment venues, malls and museums: you stand in front of a camera and see yourself in another world, with everything running locally on one GPU. The first version ran at about 5 FPS; after optimizing the pipeline and training his own LoRA, Zori Baazov reached about 10 FPS on the same GPU. The project is still in R&D.

Role
Zori Baazov (DepthLab): real-time pipeline and custom LoRA training
About the project

The brief

A client asked for a real-time AI mirror on one local GPU. The deal did not close, but the project had become interesting, so Zori kept working on it as in-house R&D.

How it works

The live camera image goes through an AI model on one local GPU, and the visitor sees the same moment in another world. The hard part is speed: video AI models render clips in minutes, and a mirror needs several new frames every second.

Zori's role and where it is going

Zori builds the real-time pipeline and trains the custom LoRA. He sees the mirror as an AI installation for entertainment venues, malls and museums, where each visitor in front of the camera sees a live image of themselves in another world.

What the gallery shows

Side by side, the camera image shows a man in an ordinary living room, and the AI version shows the same moment with the floor turned into a beach. In the clips he stands in shallow surf with his arms out, kicks at small waves and crouches to touch the water.

Type
Real-time AI installation, R&D
System
A live camera and one local GPU; a custom LoRA trained in-house
Runtime
Real time: about 5 FPS in the first version, about 10 FPS on the same GPU after optimization
